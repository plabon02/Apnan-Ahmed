Practical AI Apps You Can Use Today (Not Just ChatGPT)

Everyone knows ChatGPT — fewer people know the practical AI apps around it that do real, everyday work. Used wisely, these tools collapse hours into minutes: images, transcripts, documents and designs. Here are the categories I rely on most, plus one simple workflow that combines them all.

1. Free AI image generation

You don't need a paid subscription for decent images. Bing Image Creator (DALL·E) is free, Canva's Magic Media offers free styles, and Leonardo.Ai gives daily free tokens once you find a consistent look. Use these for hero backgrounds, social posts and mood boards before committing to final artwork.

2. AI transcription that's genuinely useful

Whisper, OpenAI's open-source model, is astonishingly accurate. For a zero-config option, Google Recorder transcribes on-device for free on Android, and Otter.ai handles meetings. Transcribe a lecture or interview and you get a searchable text draft in minutes — a huge timesaver for students and researchers. Always check privacy rules before recording people.

3. Document and presentation helpers

Word's AI drafts and summarizes documents; PowerPoint's AI turns a text outline into slides; Canva's AI can re-theme a whole presentation. Combine these with transcription and a recorded talk becomes a clean slide deck surprisingly fast.

4. AI design and canvas tools

Remove backgrounds, upscale, extend images and regenerate fill areas with tools like Photoshop's Generative Fill, Photopea's AI and Canva's background remover. They handle the tedious 80% of retouching so the human designer keeps the final judgment.

One workflow combining them

  1. Record your talk or interview (with permission) on your phone.
  2. Transcribe it with an AI transcription tool.
  3. Generate a few mood images with a free image generator.
  4. Let a presentation AI turn your transcript into slides.
  5. Polish the deck in Canva or PowerPoint, then export.
The best AI apps are the ones doing one narrow job well. Stack three of them and the day feels twice as long.

Start with a single tool this week — I'd pick transcription first. Which practical AI apps are already in your daily workflow? Drop them in the comments.
