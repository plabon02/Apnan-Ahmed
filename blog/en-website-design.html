Building a Website That Converts: My Practical Checklist

Building a website that converts is less about creativity and more about a checklist. Whether you're launching a portfolio, a small business site or a landing page, the same fundamentals decide whether visitors take action or click away. Here is my practical website design checklist, refined through years of building sites for clients.

1. Define one clear purpose

One page should push one action: buy, call, email or download. Every element either supports that decision or gets removed. Vague pages convert nothing.

2. Nail the above-the-fold message

The first screen must answer three questions in about three seconds: what you do, who it's for, and what to do next. That means a headline, a one-line subhead and a visible call to action — before any scrolling.

3. Design mobile-first

Most of your traffic will come from phones. Design the small screen first — single column, large tap targets, readable font sizes — then expand the layout for desktop. Always test on a real device, not just the browser inspector.

4. Make images fast

Heavy images are the number one killer of load speed. Use WebP, compress before upload, and lazy-load images below the fold. A fast site ranks better, bounces less and converts more.

5. Build a strong, repeated CTA

One clear button in the hero is not enough. Repeat the call to action after each section and again at the bottom, with benefit-driven copy like "Get your free quote" instead of a vague "Submit".

6. Add contact and trust signals

A real address, phone number and working email build trust. Add testimonials, case studies, guarantees and a visible security badge. On small business sites, a local contact detail alone can lift conversions sharply.

7. Measure with analytics

Install analytics from day one. Track what matters — which pages close, which buttons get clicked, where visitors drop off. A website that converts is a website that is measured and improved.

Priority order

  1. One purpose plus a strong above-the-fold message.
  2. Mobile-first layout.
  3. Fast images.
  4. Strong, repeated calls to action.
  5. Contact and trust signals.
  6. Analytics from day one.
Conversion is not a mystery; it's measurement. Build, measure, refine — that's the entire secret.

Run this website design checklist against your current site this week. Which item is weakest right now? Tell me in the comments.
